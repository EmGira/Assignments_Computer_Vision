real-world scenarios.



In this analysis we will implement the following 4 methods on the degraded images:

- **baseline**: the degraded images, wich are a result of a small gaussian blur and contrast reduction on the original images.

- **linear contrast stretching**: operating on both the BGR channels and on Luminance in the LAB format.

- **histogram_equalization**: equalizing L on LAB.

- **bilateral filter + histogram equalization (LAB)**: the idea is to reduce the noise amplification of the histogram equalization by first applying a classical denoiser, this resulted in a reduction in noise, but also noticeable smoothing of the details compared to the single histogram equalization on L.

# Numerical improvement vs. visual quality

PSNR and SSIM are full-reference metrics: they measure how close a result is to the original, not how good it looks.
A higher score therefore does not always mean a better-looking image.

## Shadows, histogram equalization (LAB)

Both metrics improve over the baseline: PSNR goes from 16.04 to 17.82 dB and SSIM from 0.593 to 0.678.
But visually the image presents noticeable issues:
- the grainy texture on the upper wall is heavily amplified
- the light patches lose their warm color and turn almost white
- the change in intensity between shadows and light is too drastic

Linear stretching gives a result much closer to the original, and it also scores higher (25.46 dB / 0.795).

why PSNR improves: the degradation pulls every pixel halfway toward 128, which halves the intensity spread, so the degraded image is wrong almost everywhere.
Equalization spreads the values out again, so most pixels move closer to their original value and the mean squared error drops.

why SSIM improves: 
the single SSIM score hides what is happening. 
SSIM compares the two images in small windows and, in each window, **multiplies** three terms:
- **luminance**: local mean brightness similarity
- **contrast**: local amount of variation (standard deviation) similarity
- **structure**: correlation in pixel values.

The degradation mainly damages the first two terms: it shifts the brightness toward mid-grey and halves the contrast, but the pattern of light and shadow stays in the same place, so the structure term remains high.

Histogram equalization repairs brightness and contrast, the two terms the degradation had damaged, so they rise a lot.
At the same time it amplifies the grain, so the structure term gets worse.

therefore SSIM, still increases, even though the structure, which is what we notice most visually, is worse than before.


# Qualitative analysis

## Shadows: linear stretching per channel (BGR)

- **Easier to see:** the edges of the shadows, the outline of the pole, the silhouette of the person and the texture of the the lower wall. The warm colours are close to the original. This method also has the best metrics.
- **Trade-offs:** the blur remains, since none of the methods sharpen the image. 
- **Other methods:** histogram equalization amplifies the grain on the wall and turns the light patches white. Applying the bilateral filter first reduces the grain, but does not fix the white lights.

## low_contrast: linear stretching on luminance (LAB)

- **Easier to see:** the folds and creases of the plastic sheet, which are barely visible in the degraded image.
- **Trade-offs:** the colours look more neutral grey than in the original. Stretching only L does not restore the colors.
- **Other methods:** per-channel stretching produces unnatural colours, a strong blue and purple cast. 
Histogram equalization shows the creases most clearly, but it amplifies the grain.
The bilateral filter reduces the grain at the cost of a loss in details.

## poor_details: linear stretching per channel (BGR)

- **Easier to see:** the lights in the image regain their contrast against the dark background, with colours closer to the original.
- **Trade-offs:** the details in the sky are lost, as well as the faint details is on the buildings, overall the image is too dark.
- **Other methods:**
  - *Luminance stretching (LAB)* keeps a little more of the dark detail, but the colours look faded: The degradation pulled the colours toward grey, and stretching only L restores the brightness but not the colour.
  - *Histogram equalization (LAB)* fails on this image. Equalization tries to spread the pixels evenly over all Luminance levels. Here most of the image is dark, which in the degraded image results in mostly a the same L level. It highlights some details on the left, but at the cost of the rest of the image.
  - *Bilateral filter + histogram equalization (LAB)*: also fails, because the problem is not the noise but the way equalization redistributes a histogram dominated by one value.
